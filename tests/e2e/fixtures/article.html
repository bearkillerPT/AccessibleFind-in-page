The Lighthouse at Cabo da Roca

A short walk along Europe’s westernmost cliffs · 6 min read

At the edge of the continent, where the cliffs drop into the Atlantic, a red-topped lighthouse has guided ships since 1772. The keepers who lived here climbed its stairs every evening to light the lamp.

Today the lighthouse is automated, but the view from the cape is unchanged. On clear days you can follow the coast north for kilometres; on foggy ones the beam is the only thing you can see.

Walk south from the lighthouse along the cliff path and, after twenty minutes, you reach a small cove. Fishermen still bring their boats in here at dawn, and the café at the top of the steps serves grilled sardines at lunch.

Visitors often ask why the lighthouse sits so far back from the edge. The answer is erosion: the original tower stood closer to the sea, and the cliff beneath it has been retreating for centuries.

Before you leave, look for the stone monument quoting the poet Camões: “Where the land ends and the sea begins.”
